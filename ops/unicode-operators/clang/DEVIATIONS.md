### ucd-version-drift

**Formerly:** `DEV-U01`. **Status:** **RECONCILED** — 2026-09-06, [reconcile-declaring-using](../../completion/steps/reconcile-declaring-using.md), into `docs/unicode-operators.md` **U§4**, the new paragraph beginning *"Two Unicode versions are in play at once"* (immediately **before** *"One more consequence of R3c worth stating"*, and immediately **after** the reproducibility paragraph `evidence-debt` added — both are wanted and neither replaces the other), and **U§7.1**, the first bullet of *"First, the conflict cannot arise in C++ today"*, in the sentences beginning *"It has been checked at two Unicode versions"*. The separation the row asked to have stated is stated as the design rather than as an apology: the operator list is frozen at 17.0 **by the proposal** and moves only by an explicit act of a future revision, while an implementation's identifier tables ride Unicode's schedule — and the skew is what makes the disjointness check stronger than a single-version one. **Re-derived rather than inherited, 2026-09-06:** `clang/lib/Lex/UnicodeCharSets.h` on `unicode-operators-upstream` still labels `XIDStartRanges`, `XIDContinueRanges` and both `MathematicalNotationProfileID*Ranges` **Unicode 18.0**, and `UnicodeOperatorCharSets.h` still says 17.0.0 / 1,381 code points.

**Found by.** U00 (for U02/U05)

**Design section.** U§4, U§7.1, [token-set](../../../docs/unicode-operators.md#token-set), [operator-identifier-disjointness](../../../docs/unicode-operators.md#operator-identifier-disjointness)

**What the design said.** Everything is derived and checked against **UCD 17.0.0** / UAX #31 revision 43 — including the [operator-identifier-disjointness](../../../docs/unicode-operators.md#operator-identifier-disjointness) claim "Pattern_Syntax ∩ XID_Start = Pattern_Syntax ∩ XID_Continue = ∅ in UCD 17.0" and the Clang math-identifier composition argument.

**What was true.** Clang trunk's in-tree tables have already moved to **Unicode 18.0**: `clang/lib/Lex/UnicodeCharSets.h` labels `XIDStartRanges`, `XIDContinueRanges`, `MathematicalNotationProfileIDStartRanges` and `…ContinueRanges` "Unicode 18.0". So U02's in-tree disjointness cross-check runs [token-set](../../../docs/unicode-operators.md#token-set)@17.0 against XID@18.0 — a *stronger* check if it passes, but not the one the doc claims to have made. (The `ID_Compat_Math_Start` table does contain exactly ∂ U+2202, ∇ U+2207, ∞ U+221E plus 10 Mathematical-Italic variants, as U10 asserts.)

**Recommended doc change.** Note in U§4 that the frozen [token-set](../../../docs/unicode-operators.md#token-set) set is pinned to UCD 17.0 **by the proposal** while implementations track whatever UCD their identifier tables use — that separation is the point of [token-set](../../../docs/unicode-operators.md#token-set), but the disjointness claim in U§7.1 / [operator-identifier-disjointness](../../../docs/unicode-operators.md#operator-identifier-disjointness) should be re-stated as verified at 17.0 *and* 18.0 once U02 measures it. If U02's cross-check finds any overlap at 18.0, that is a U10 finding, not a table bug. **Measured by U02 (2026-08-03): no overlap — see [disjointness-evidence](#disjointness-evidence).**

### disjointness-evidence

**Formerly:** `DEV-U02`. **Status:** **RECONCILED** — 2026-09-06, [reconcile-declaring-using](../../completion/steps/reconcile-declaring-using.md), into `docs/unicode-operators.md` **U§7.1**, the first bullet of *"First, the conflict cannot arise in C++ today"* (the added sentences from *"It has been checked at two Unicode versions"* to the end of the bullet), with the version-skew framing in the **U§4** paragraph named under [ucd-version-drift](#ucd-version-drift). The claim is stated as *verified at 17.0 and against an 18.0 identifier set*, per-code-point over all 1,381 members, against XID_Start, XID_Continue, the math-identifier profile and the C++11–C++20 whitelist. **Re-run rather than quoted, 2026-09-06:** the in-tree unit test that makes the sweep is `clang/unittests/Lex/UnicodeOperatorCharSetsTest.cpp`, and all 14 of its cases pass on the pre-built `unicode-operators-upstream` binary — including `UserOperatorsAreNeverIdentifierChars` and `TightestGapAgainstMathIdentifiers`. That the check is a *test* and not an argument is now said in U§7.1, because it is the difference between a claim a reviewer must take on trust and one a reviewer can run.

**Found by.** U02

**Design section.** U§7.1, [operator-identifier-disjointness](../../../docs/unicode-operators.md#operator-identifier-disjointness) (resolves the [ucd-version-drift](#ucd-version-drift) open question)

**What the design said.** Disjointness of the operator set and identifier space is asserted against UCD 17.0 only ("Pattern_Syntax ∩ XID_Start = Pattern_Syntax ∩ XID_Continue = ∅ in UCD 17.0").

**What was true.** Measured in-tree, per code point, over all 1,381 [token-set](../../../docs/unicode-operators.md#token-set) members against Clang's own tables — which are labelled **Unicode 18.0**: **zero** are XID_Start, zero XID_Continue, zero in `MathematicalNotationProfileID{Start,Continue}Ranges` (the D137051/P3658R1 math-identifier profile), zero in the C++11–C++20 `C11AllowedIDCharRanges` whitelist. The [token-set](../../../docs/unicode-operators.md#token-set)@17.0 set stayed out of identifier space *after* the UCD moved under it.

**Recommended doc change.** U§7.1 can strengthen its claim: the partition is verified at 17.0 **and** holds against a 18.0 identifier set, which is the version-skew case a real implementation actually faces (a compiler updates its XID tables on Unicode's schedule while the frozen operator list does not move). Worth one sentence in U§7.1 and a note in U§4's "immutable versus stable" — the paper's strongest empirical answer to "what happens when Unicode updates?".

### exclusion-list-derivation

**Formerly:** `DEV-U03`. **Status:** OPEN

**Found by.** U02

**Design section.** U§5 predicate 5 (the named exclusion list)

**What the design said.** The exclusion list is given as prose: "∂ ∇ ∞", then "U+2212 −, U+2215 ∕ and U+2044 ⁄, U+2217 ∗, U+2223 ∣, U+2236 ∶, U+22C5 ⋅ **and the middle-dot family**, U+2264/U+2265 ≤ ≥ and … ⇐ ⇒ ⇔", then "anything with emoji presentation".

**What was true.** An implementation must enumerate it, and three things had to be decided to do that. (a) "The middle-dot family" is not a UCD family: inside the [token-set](../../../docs/unicode-operators.md#token-set) blocks it resolves to exactly **U+2219 ∙ BULLET OPERATOR** and **U+22C5 ⋅ DOT OPERATOR** — U+00B7 MIDDLE DOT itself is **not Pattern_Syntax** (Unicode withheld it precisely because it is used *in identifiers*, e.g. Catalan), so it was never a candidate. (b) **U+2044 ⁄ FRACTION SLASH is not in the [token-set](../../../docs/unicode-operators.md#token-set) blocks** (it is U+2044, the blocks start at U+2190), so it already fails predicate 3; listing it under predicate 5 is harmless but misleading — of the 16 named exclusions it is the only one that would not otherwise have qualified. (c) U§8 wants the exclusions to carry the token they ape, but the list assigns none per character; U02 assigned them: − → `-`, ∕ ⁄ → `/`, ∗ → `*`, ∣ → `

**Recommended doc change.** `, ∶ → `:`, ∙ ⋅ → `.` (they are dot-shaped; the *visual* confusion is with `.`, not with the `*` their mathematics means), ≤ ⇐ → `<=`, ≥ → `>=`, ⇒ → `=>`, ⇔ → `<=>` (C++20 has a token that shape). The emoji-presentation exclusion resolves to **12** code points inside the blocks (⌚ ⌛ ⏩ ⏪ ⏫ ⏬ ⏰ ⏳ ⬛ ⬜ ⭐ ⭕).

### declaration-name-plumbing

**Formerly:** `DEV-U04`. **Status:** **RECONCILED** — 2026-09-06, [reconcile-implementation-cost](../../completion/steps/reconcile-implementation-cost.md), into `docs/unicode-operators.md` **U§8**, the ***The name tables — `DeclarationName`*** bullet (fourth in the Clang list). The bullet keeps the worked-precedent reassurance, because it held, and adds the three things this row asked for: the quantification, the *packed against Objective-C's variable-length selector encoding* finding as the real sharp edge, and the generated TableGen site. **The count was re-measured and is 34 dispatch sites across 17 files, not 33 across 20** — 31 `case` arms in 15 files, one TableGen `PropertyTypeCase` generating the abstract reader/writer pair, and two non-`switch` `||` chains. The 33/20 figure was a correct snapshot at `U06`; later steps added sites. The `||` chains are a finding in their own right and are written up in [dispatch-obligation-taxonomy](../../../docs/unicode-operators.md#dispatch-obligation-taxonomy). The serialization *identity dividend* [serialization-tooling-cost](#serialization-tooling-cost) asked to be stated in this same paragraph is in it.

**Found by.** U06

**Design section.** U§8 "Clang — *The hard part — `DeclarationName`*"

**What the design said.** "Overloaded operators are `CXXOperatorName` over the closed `OverloadedOperatorKind` enum… User operators need a new `DeclarationName` kind carrying the code point. The precedent is **`CXXLiteralOperatorName`**… This is a real cost — DeclarationName plumbing fans out — but it is a *worked* precedent, not terra incognita."

**What was true.** **The precedent held exactly, and the cost is smaller than the sentence implies — but the sharp edge is somewhere else entirely.** Measured: **33** switch sites over `DeclarationName::NameKind` across 20 files; the whole step is **21 files, +436/−1 lines**, of which **19 files / +249 lines** are production code and 186 lines are the new unittest. `CXXLiteralOperatorIdName`'s shape transplants almost line for line. What the sketch does not say: (a) the new kind is not a *choice* — the inline 3-bit `StoredNameKind` space is **completely full** (all eight values 0–7 taken), so any new kind is *forced* through `DeclarationNameExtra`; the literal-operator route is the only route. (b) `DeclarationNameExtra::ExtraKind` **cannot be appended to**: the single field `ExtraKindOrNumArgs` encodes an N-argument Objective-C selector as `ObjCMultiArgSelector + N`, and `getKind()` clamps every value ≥ `ObjCMultiArgSelector` back to it, so appending `CXXUserOperatorName` after it would silently misclassify every multi-keyword selector. It must be *inserted before* `ObjCMultiArgSelector`, renumbering `ExtraKind` 3→4 and `NameKind::ObjCMultiArgSelector` 11→12. (c) One of the 33 sites is **generated**, from `clang/include/clang/AST/PropertiesBase.td` into `AbstractBasicReader/Writer.inc` — it is not greppable as C++ and only the `-Wswitch` warning finds it.

**Recommended doc change.** U§8's `DeclarationName` paragraph should quantify rather than hedge: "a real cost" becomes "~250 lines across 19 files and 33 switch sites, one of them TableGen-generated" — a number the paper can defend, and small enough to be an argument *for* the design rather than a caveat. Then replace the "worked precedent" reassurance with the real finding: the operator-name space is not merely closed, it is **packed against Objective-C's variable-length selector encoding**, so opening it costs an enum renumbering in a shared header rather than a new enumerator. That is the sentence a committee reader wants — it says exactly how much of the difficulty is inherent to open-ended operator names (little) and how much is an accident of one compiler's bit-packing (most of it), and it is the kind of evidence GCC's `ansi_opname` half of U§8 should be measured against in the same units.

### declaring-side-parse-cost

**Formerly:** `DEV-U05`. **Status:** **RECONCILED** — 2026-09-06, [reconcile-implementation-cost](../../completion/steps/reconcile-implementation-cost.md), into `docs/unicode-operators.md` **U§8**, the new ***Parser, declaring an operator*** bullet, which is the split this row asked for; the *using* bullet above it is the old Parser bullet, kept and marked as having held. Both findings landed: the tentative-parsing arm with the two-for-two generalisation, and the `TemplateIdAnnotation` gap. **The peer-site count was re-measured as 12 dispatch sites over `UnqualifiedIdKind` in 5 files, not 9**, and the row's *"all exhaustive, so `-Wswitch` found them"* is true of only 7 of the 12: the other five are `==` tests, which nothing checks. That correction is not cosmetic — it is one of the three instances of the *spelling, not the axis* finding in [dispatch-obligation-taxonomy](../../../docs/unicode-operators.md#dispatch-obligation-taxonomy). The row's ordering claim survives: the declaring side is smaller than the name-table cost, as the design predicts, and U§8 now says so. **U§6's contested sentence is not touched here** — it is [reconcile-remainder](../../completion/steps/reconcile-remainder.md)'s; U§8's opening paragraph carries the U§8-side evidence and the caveat.

**Found by.** U07

**Design section.** U§8 "Clang" bullet list; U§6's "parsing is the *easy* part of this feature, easier even than backtick"; U§7 "Declaring"

**What the design said.** U§8's three Clang bullets are *Lexer*, *Parser* ("the `prec::Level` introduced for backtick serves as-is; a `tok::user_operator` case joins `tok::backtick` in `ParseRHSOfBinaryExpression`, and a case in `ParseCastExpression` handles the prefix form") and *the hard part — `DeclarationName`*. U§7 gives the declaring side one sentence: "*operator-function-id* grows one production… Everything downstream is the existing machinery."

**What was true.** **The headline claim holds — and the omitted half is now measured.** U§8's Parser bullet describes only *expression* parsing (U11/U12); the *declaring* side, which is where `operator⊞` becomes a name at all, is a separate and slightly larger piece of parser work: **8 production files, +89/−5 lines**, namely a new `UnqualifiedIdKind` with its payload and setter, one arm in `ParseUnqualifiedIdOperator`, one arm in `Sema::GetNameFromUnqualifiedId`, and **9 peer sites** that had to learn the kind — four template-id paths in `ParseExprCXX.cpp`, `Sema::isTemplateName`, and four `UnqualifiedIdKind` switches in `SemaDecl.cpp`/`SemaType.cpp`/`SemaDeclCXX.cpp` (all exhaustive, so `-Wswitch` found them; the build is warning-clean). Two findings inside that: (a) **tentative parsing needed a peer change the design does not anticipate.** `Parser::TryParseOperatorId` handles `operator new/delete`, the `OperatorKinds.def` punctuators, `()`, `[]`, a literal-operator-id, and *otherwise assumes a conversion-type-id follows*; a `tok::user_operator` fell through to that loop, found no decl-specifier, and returned `TPResult::Error`, so an ambiguous statement such as `S operator⊛(S, S);` was re-parsed as an expression and rejected. One four-line case fixes it. The backtick track needed a peer change in the *same file* at its S08, for an unrelated reason — so "tentative parsing has to be told about any new declarator-id token" is a two-for-two result across the two features, not a coincidence. (b) `TemplateIdAnnotation` has no slot for a code point, exactly as it has none for a literal-operator suffix (the pre-existing `// FIXME: Store name for literal operator too.`), so `operator⊞<T>` parses and resolves through the `TemplateName` while the annotation carries `nullptr`/`OO_None`. Upstream's one-line `OpKind` expression also *reads the wrong union member* for a literal-operator-id; U07 had to make that read kind-checked rather than inherit the bug for a `uint32_t` payload.

**Recommended doc change.** Split U§8's Parser bullet in two — *declaring* and *using* — and give the declaring half the measurement (~90 lines, 8 files, one new `UnqualifiedId` kind, 9 peer sites, all compiler-found) next to U§7's "grows one production". It supports U§6's claim rather than undercutting it: the parse cost really is small, and it is *smaller than the `DeclarationName` cost* ([declaration-name-plumbing](#declaration-name-plumbing)'s ~250 lines / 33 sites), which is the ordering the design predicts. Then add the tentative-parsing sentence as a portable implementation note — "a compiler that disambiguates declarations from expressions by trial parse must teach that path the new token too; both Clang prototypes needed it" — because it is the one place the declaring side is *not* just "the existing machinery", and GCC's `cp_parser_*` tentative machinery will face the same question.

### over-oper-restrictions

**Formerly:** `DEV-U06`. **Status:** **RECONCILED** — 2026-09-06, [reconcile-declaring-using](../../completion/steps/reconcile-declaring-using.md), into `docs/unicode-operators.md` **U§7 "Declaring"**: the five-row table of [over.oper] restrictions with what happens to each, the paragraph after it that states the generalization as a rule (*"[over.oper]'s restrictions protect a token whose parse, arity and fixity the grammar has already fixed"*), and the closing paragraph *"The one restriction kept is kept as a choice"*. The static-member choice has the decision entry this row asked for: **[static-member-operators](../../../docs/unicode-operators.md#static-member-operators)**, a new §2 entry in the full Question / Status / Decision / Why / Decided by / Log shape, sited between [unary-forms](../../../docs/unicode-operators.md#unary-forms) and [candidate-assembly](../../../docs/unicode-operators.md#candidate-assembly); [operator-function-id](../../../docs/unicode-operators.md#operator-function-id) gains a `Log.` line recording that the enumeration is written. **The variadic waiver was re-derived, not taken from this row:** `int operator⊞(int, int, ...)` is accepted by the 2026-09-06 `unicode-operators-upstream` build, and the doc says why it is inert (operator syntax cannot pass a trailing argument). The **paper** carried this row's superseded reason in its own words — *"the arity rule presupposes an implicit object parameter, and a static member has none"* — and that bullet in `papers/dxxxxr0.md` is corrected in the same commit; leaving it would have shipped the reason the answer discarded. The decision below stands unchanged. — *Previously:* **OPEN — DECIDED 2026-09-06.** [over-oper-restrictions](../../../docs/open-decisions.md#over-oper-restrictions), option (a): static member user operators stay **rejected**, and (b) and (c) stay **waived**. The reason changes, and this is the substantive part of the answer — **this row's stated basis does not survive inspection**. "A static member has no implicit object parameter, so it can name neither form" is not what the code does: `CheckUserOperatorDeclaration` computes `NumOperands = NumDeclaredParams + (HasImplicitObjectParam ? 1 : 0)` and accepts 1 or 2, so `static S operator⊞(S, S)` has `NumOperands == 2` and the arity rule alone would accept it; it is rejected only by the `MD->isStatic()` guard that runs three lines earlier. The restriction is a **choice**, not a consequence. Its reason is that the desugaring equivalence is defined over exactly two spellings — `operator⊞(x, y)` and `x.operator⊞(y)` — and a static member names neither (`x.operator⊞(y)` on a static member is legal C++ but passes one argument to a two-parameter function). Still `OPEN` because U§7 "Declaring" is unwritten and the choice is owed a decision entry of its own (suggested slug `static-member-operators`, named for the question). Owed by [reconcile-declaring-using](../../completion/steps/reconcile-declaring-using.md).

**Found by.** U08

**Design section.** U§7 "Declaring"; [operator-function-id](../../../docs/unicode-operators.md#operator-function-id), [unary-forms](../../../docs/unicode-operators.md#unary-forms)

**What the design said.** "*operator-function-id* grows one production… Everything downstream is the existing machinery: free function or member, any parameter types, templates, `constexpr`, `= delete`, the lot. Arity selects the form ([unary-forms](../../../docs/unicode-operators.md#unary-forms))… Unlike the existing operators there is **no class-or-enum parameter requirement** ([operator-function-id](../../../docs/unicode-operators.md#operator-function-id))." The only named departure from [over.oper] is the class-or-enum rule.

**What was true.** **"The existing machinery" is the wrong frame for the declaring rules, and the class-or-enum rule is not the only [over.oper] rule that must not carry over.** [over.oper] imposes five restrictions on an operator function, and a user operator inherits exactly *one* of them. Measured against `Sema::CheckOverloadedOperatorDeclaration`: (a) **class-or-enum parameter** — waived, as [operator-function-id](../../../docs/unicode-operators.md#operator-function-id) says; (b) **no default arguments** ([over.oper]p8, `err_operator_overload_default_arg`) — must also be waived, and the design never says so; (c) **not variadic** (`err_operator_overload_variadic`) — likewise waived; (d) **the arity table** (`OperatorUses[Op]` from `OperatorKinds.def`) — has no entry to consult, so the prefix/infix rule has to be written out; (e) **not a static member** (`err_operator_overload_static`) — *kept*, and this one is a judgement U08 had to make with no guidance from the design: [unary-forms](../../../docs/unicode-operators.md#unary-forms)'s arity rule is stated as "two parameters, or one as a member", which presupposes an implicit object parameter, and a static member function has none, so it can name neither form. U08 rejects it, reusing the existing diagnostic. Structurally, the consequence is that `CheckUserOperatorDeclaration` is a **sibling** of `CheckOverloadedOperatorDeclaration`, not a widening of it — 54 lines that share no code with it — and that is also what makes "no existing operator's rules moved" provable rather than merely tested (the shared checker was never touched; the class-or-enum branch is unreachable from the new path rather than conditionally skipped).

**Recommended doc change.** Rewrite U§7 "Declaring" to enumerate the five [over.oper] restrictions and say which survive, rather than naming only the class-or-enum one. The generalization is sharper than the current sentence and worth stating as such: **[over.oper]'s restrictions exist to protect a token whose parse, arity and fixity the grammar already fixed; a user operator's only fixed property is arity, so arity is the only rule it inherits.** Default arguments and variadic parameter lists then fall out as *allowed* by derivation rather than by fiat. Add the static-member decision to the decisions log as a sub-point of [unary-forms](../../../docs/unicode-operators.md#unary-forms) (or a new U-number) with its rationale — it is a small, real, litigable choice, and the C++23 `static operator()` precedent means someone will ask whether a static member user operator should be admitted as a second infix form. Also worth one sentence for the paper's implementation section: the checker is a sibling, not an extension, and that is why the relaxation provably cannot leak into `operator+`.

**What the design said.** "the Itanium **vendor-extended operator** production, `v <digit> <source-name>` … e.g. binary `⊞` mangles as `v2` plus a code-point-derived source-name such as `op_u229E`, giving stable, demangler-tolerated symbols for the fork", with the two productions "disjoint by the mangling grammar itself".

Note the padding and the widening are both **unexercised by construction today**: every [token-set](../../../docs/unicode-operators.md#token-set) code point is in 0x2190–0x2BFF, so every derived name is exactly four digits. (b) **"Demangler-tolerated" understates the result.** Measured: `llvm-cxxfilt` *and* **GNU binutils `c++filt` 2.46** — a different vendor's demangler, unmodified — both render the `v` production readably in every form tested, including nested-name, const-qualified member, C++23 explicit-object member and template-id: `_Zv28op_u229E1SS_` → `operator op_u229E(S, S)`, `_ZNK1Tv28op_u229EES_` → `T::operator op_u229E(T) const`, `_Zv28op_u22A0IiEiT_S0_` → `int operator op_u22A0<int>(int, int)`, `_ZNH1Ev28op_u2297ES_S_` → `E::operator op_u2297(this E, E)`. Existing toolchains need **no change** to inspect these symbols. (c) **The arity digit is stable where linking uses it and follows the expression elsewhere — and that is upstream's shape, not this feature's.** In `<base-unresolved-name>` position (a dependent `decltype(t.operator⊞(t))`) the digit is the *call's* argument count, so a member infix operator whose definition mangles `v2` mangles `on v1 …` there. Upstream does exactly the same for the built-in operators — `decltype(t.operator+(t))` mangles `onps`, i.e. **unary** plus, for a binary member `operator+` — so it is inherited behavior, invisible to linkage (the defining symbol is unaffected), and bug-compatible with what a demangler already expects.

**Recommended doc change.** Give U§9 the derivation **rule** rather than the example, in the wording above; it is the sentence the paper will be quoted on and the sentence an ABI group would review. Upgrade "demangler-tolerated" to the measurement — *two* independent demanglers, one of them binutils, render every form with no changes — because that is the strongest thing the prototype can say about the `v` production and it is exactly the question an ABI reviewer asks first. U§7 "Desugaring": "The result *is* the call `operator⊞(x, y)` (member form: `x.operator⊞(y)`), so [desugaring-target](../../../docs/backtick-operator-design.md#desugaring-target)'s inheritance list — overload resolution, ADL, templates, SFINAE, constexpr, conversions, value categories, codegen — … carry over **without modification**." U§8 lists three Clang work items (Lexer, Parser, `DeclarationName`); there is no fourth.

**What was true.** **Three findings, and the third is the one for the paper.** **(1) "Exactly as an overloaded operator does" cannot be implemented by calling what an overloaded operator calls — but the un-reusable part is one six-line loop.** `Sema::CreateOverloadedBinOp`/`CreateOverloadedUnaryOp` are keyed on `OverloadedOperatorKind` end to end, so U13 is a *sibling*, `Sema::CreateOverloadedUserOp`, exactly as U08's `CheckUserOperatorDeclaration` is a sibling of `CheckOverloadedOperatorDeclaration` (two for two). Measured, helper by helper: **reusable because keyed on a `DeclarationName` or a `DeclAccessPair`** — `AddNonMemberOperatorCandidates`, `AddArgumentDependentLookupCandidates`, `AddMethodCandidate`, `OverloadCandidateSet::BestViableFunction`, `NoteCandidates`, `BuildCallExpr`, `BuildMemberReferenceExpr`, and the whole `err_ovl_*_call` diagnostic family. **Not reusable** — `AddMemberOperatorCandidates`, whose *first line* is `getCXXOperatorName(Op)` and whose remaining six lines are copied verbatim; `AddBuiltinOperatorCandidates`, which is not wanted; and `CXXOperatorCallExpr`, which stores the kind in the node. The whole step is **3 production files, +207/−40**, of which ~60 lines are comment and the genuinely new logic is candidate assembly and nothing else. **(2) The absence of built-in candidates is a one-line absence, and it is now stated.** Nothing calls `AddBuiltinOperatorCandidates`; `1 ⊠ 2` with nothing declared is `use of undeclared 'operator⊠'`, `a ⊟ b` with only `operator⊟(Base, Base)` visible is `no matching function for call to 'operator⊟'`, and `p ⊞ n` on `int *` is the same rather than pointer arithmetic. [candidate-assembly](../../../docs/unicode-operators.md#candidate-assembly) needed no mechanism at all — which is worth saying in the paper as *the* structural difference from `operator+`. **(3) The operator-ness of a use has to live in the AST node, or two-phase lookup loses the member candidates.** Because `CXXOperatorCallExpr` is welded to `OverloadedOperatorKind`, a user-operator use is an ordinary `CallExpr` (non-member) or `CXXMemberCallExpr` (member) — which is precisely U§7's desugaring, and is *good* for the non-dependent case. But when an operand is type-dependent the expression is a `CallExpr` with an unresolved callee, and `TreeTransform` rebuilds it at instantiation through `ActOnCallExpr`, which assembles **call** candidates ([over.match.call]) and not **operator** candidates ([over.match.oper]). ADL survives, because ADL is a property of the *call*; member candidates do not, because they are a property of the *operator syntax*, and the callee's `DeclarationName` records the operator's identity but not the fact that operator syntax was used. Measured: `template <class T> constexpr auto f(T a, T b) { return a ⊕ b; }` with a member `operator⊕` gives `use of undeclared 'operator⊕'` at instantiation, and `requires(T a, T b) { a ⊕ b; }` is unsatisfied for that `T`. Non-dependent member uses work in every shape tested (inherited members, member vs non-member ranked in one set both ways, ADL with a non-empty member set, cv/ref-qualified and explicit-object members, member templates, access and deleted diagnostics).

**Recommended doc change.** Add a **fourth Clang bullet to U§8 — *the expression node*** — and give it the finding: Clang's `CXXOperatorCallExpr` exists exactly to record that a call was written with operator syntax, `TreeTransform` reads the operator kind back off it to re-run operator candidate assembly at instantiation, and it is keyed on `OverloadedOperatorKind`. So the closure of the operator tables is not confined to `DeclarationName` ([declaration-name-plumbing](#declaration-name-plumbing)): it reaches the expression node, and the expression node is where two-phase lookup reads it from. That is a second, independent instance of the same thesis and is stronger evidence than the name-table one, because here the closure has an observable *language* consequence rather than a plumbing cost. Then soften U§7 "Desugaring"'s "without modification": the desugaring is exact for a non-dependent use and needs a node to survive a dependent one — an implementation note a replaying implementer must have. In U§7 "Using", say explicitly that the three candidate sources form **one** set ranked together, not three passes; U13 proves it both ways (`MN{} ⊘ 0` picks the non-member, `MN{} ⊘ 0L` picks the member) and it is the observable that distinguishes this design from a member-first fallback. Finally, U§7's "no built-in candidates" sentence can be strengthened from a rule to a non-mechanism: nothing implements it.

(c) **The upstream node that does have this job is `CXXRewrittenBinaryOperator`, and it is a closer model than `CXXOperatorCallExpr`.** It exists for exactly the same reason — record that an expression was *written* one way and *means* another, so instantiation can redo the resolution rather than replay the result — and its site list transplanted one for one. **Recommended doc change.** Add the fourth Clang bullet to U§8 — ***the expression node*** — with (a) and (b) above, and say the quiet part: the operator-name tables being closed is not only a plumbing cost in `DeclarationName` ([declaration-name-plumbing](#declaration-name-plumbing)) but reaches the *expression node*, and the expression node is where two-phase lookup reads operator-ness back off. That is the strongest single piece of evidence the prototype produces for the "tables are closed" thesis, because here the closure changes what programs *mean* rather than how much code it takes. Then soften U§7 "Desugaring": the desugaring is exact for a non-dependent use and needs a node to survive a dependent one — say that the node is what makes `x ⊞ y` inside a template as capable as outside one, and that the node's contents are the code point, the arity and one location, which is the whole of the "operator-ness" a use has to carry. It is worth adding, against U§6, that this is the one place the Unicode feature is *more* work than backtick rather than less: backtick's wrapper can be transparent because its meaning is the call; this one cannot, and the reason is that a user operator has member candidates and a backtick slot does not. Finally, U§8 should record the three-for-three sibling pattern as a portable finding — every place C++ keys operator behaviour off a closed kind, opening it costs a *parallel* implementation, never a widened one, and that is why the relaxation provably cannot leak into `operator+`.

### serialization-tooling-cost

**Formerly:** `DEV-U14`. **Status:** **RECONCILED** — 2026-09-06, [reconcile-implementation-cost](../../completion/steps/reconcile-implementation-cost.md), into `docs/unicode-operators.md` **U§8**, the new ***Serialization, modules and tooling*** bullet (sixth in the Clang list), with two pieces placed elsewhere as this row asked: the *identity dividend* is in the `DeclarationName` bullet, and the `hasAnyOperatorName` refusal is the fourth row of [closed-table-sibling-pattern](../../../docs/unicode-operators.md#closed-table-sibling-pattern). **Found by.** U17

**Design section.** U§8's Clang bullet list (the serialization/tooling cost the sketch does not mention); [declaration-name-plumbing](#declaration-name-plumbing)'s 33-site count; [expression-node-cost](#expression-node-cost)'s 28-site count

**What the design said.** U§8 says the `DeclarationName` cost is "a real cost — DeclarationName plumbing fans out — but it is a *worked* precedent", and says nothing at all about what a new name kind or a new expression node owes to **serialization, module lookup, AST import or the matcher/tooling layer**. [declaration-name-plumbing](#declaration-name-plumbing) counted 33 name-kind switch sites and [expression-node-cost](#expression-node-cost) counted 28 node dispatch sites, and both treated their lists as complete.

**What was true.** **Three findings.** **(1) The two lists were complete, and the residue is tiny — but the residue is the part that is silent when wrong.** Of [declaration-name-plumbing](#declaration-name-plumbing)'s 33 name sites, five were deferred whole to this step (the `DeclarationNameKey` group: writer key length, writer key bytes, reader key, the `DeclarationNameKey` constructor, the stable hash) because they are *one* decision, not five: they are the on-disk encoding of a name in a `DeclContext`'s lookup table and its hash, and any disagreement among them makes module lookup **miss silently** rather than fail. The decision is the obvious one — the Unicode scalar value as a fixed-width `uint32`, `KeyLen += 4` — and it is 88 production lines in total for the whole step across nine files, of which the key group is ~25. Nothing needs remapping across module files, which is a property the *choice of identity* bought: [declaration-name-plumbing](#declaration-name-plumbing) chose a raw code point over an `IdentifierInfo *` for three reasons, and "the on-disk key is context-independent, so no cross-module remapping table is needed" is a fourth that only appeared here. Contrast the identifier- and selector-keyed kinds, whose key is a module-local ID that the reader has to translate. **(2) The node's serialization was written by U16 because the *linker* forced it, and it was correct on the first run — but nothing had ever executed it.** `ASTStmtWriter`/`ASTStmtReader` declare one visitor per node from `StmtNodes.inc`, so an omitted definition is a link error; U16 therefore shipped a reader and a writer whose only evidence was that they compiled. U17's PCH round trip — `-ast-print` of a TU built against a precompiled header, diffed byte-for-byte against `-ast-print` of the same source parsed whole — passed on its first execution. That is a good outcome and a bad process, and it is the clearest instance in the whole track of [expression-node-cost](#expression-node-cost)'s "the toolchain forces half a node's obligations and is silent about the rest": here the toolchain forced the *code* and was silent about its *correctness*. **(3) The tooling surface is real, unforced, and partly out of reach on principle.** ASTMatchers has no per-node requirement at all — a new node is simply invisible to it — so `userOperatorExpr()` (declaration, `VariadicDynCastAllOfMatcher`, `Registry.cpp` entry, and the *generated* `LibASTMatchersReference.html` block, which `clang/test/AST/ast_matchers_updated.test` enforces) plus two `ASTMatchFinder.cpp` traversal sites are additions nothing would have complained about omitting. **`hasAnyOperatorName()` cannot be extended to the node**, and the reason is the same closed-table thesis in a fourth place: `getOpName` returns a `StringRef` into a *static* spelling table (`OperatorKinds.def`, `BinaryOperator::getOpcodeStr`), and a user operator's spelling is a UTF-8 encoding of a code point that has to be computed into a buffer. A matcher over user operators has to be keyed on the code point, not on a name. Finally, one more site was found **only by reading the build log**: `clang/tools/libclang/CXCursor.cpp:175`'s `-Wswitch`, which U16's build did not surface because `ninja clang` had not relinked libclang at that point — bringing [expression-node-cost](#expression-node-cost)'s "found only by `-Wswitch`" category from 1 to 2 and its silent category to 13.

**Recommended doc change.** Add a fifth item to U§8's Clang list — **serialization, modules and tooling** — and give it the shape rather than a number, because the number is small and the shape is the finding: a new operator-name kind owes a `DeclContext` lookup-table key and a stable hash that must agree with it (five sites, one decision, silent when wrong), and a new expression node owes a reader, a writer, an importer, two profilers and a matcher-traversal pair, of which **only the reader and writer are forced by the build**. Then state the identity dividend explicitly in U§8's `DeclarationName` paragraph: keying the name on the *code point* rather than on an interned spelling means the on-disk key needs no cross-module remapping, which is a concrete serialization benefit of the choice [declaration-name-plumbing](#declaration-name-plumbing) made for other reasons. For the ODR question the paper can now say something flat: two declarations of the same `operator⊞` in two translation units of one module merge silently and two different ones are diagnosed, and both fall out of hashing the code point — `ODRHash::AddDeclarationNameInfo` for the name and `StmtProfiler::VisitUserOperatorExpr` (which `Stmt::ProcessODRHash` reuses) for a use. Finally, the `hasAnyOperatorName` finding belongs beside the three-for-three sibling pattern in [expression-node-cost](#expression-node-cost): it is a *fourth* instance, and the first one where the right answer is not a sibling but a refusal — the tooling predicate is keyed on a fixed spelling and a user operator does not have one.

**Found by.** U12

**Design section.** [unary-forms](../../../docs/unicode-operators.md#unary-forms); U§6 (the `unary-expression` production and its "parsing is the *easy* part" claim); U§7 "Declaring"; [over-oper-restrictions](#over-oper-restrictions)(b)

**What the design said.** [unary-forms](../../../docs/unicode-operators.md#unary-forms): "**Unary prefix** operators are declared with one parameter; prefix vs infix is disambiguated by grammatical position; **no postfix forms**… Arity selects the form, as it does for `operator-` today… Declining postfix eliminates the prefix/postfix ambiguity that forces Swift's whitespace-sensitivity rules." U§6 gives the production `unary-expression: user-operator cast-expression` and asserts "the expression grammar strictly alternates operand and operator positions, so the two never coincide".

**What was true.** **Three findings, one confirming and two qualifying.** (1) **Position alone sufficed, exactly as claimed, and the reason is structural rather than lucky.** The two productions are read by two different parser functions that never see the same token: `Parser::ParseCastExpression` runs only in operand position, and `getBinOpPrecedence` is consulted only from `ParseRHSOfBinaryExpression`, which runs only after an operand has been parsed. No lookahead, no whitespace rule, no declaration lookup, and no `bool` suppression flag of the `BacktickIsOperator`/`GreaterThanIsOperator` kind. `⊖a ⊖ b` — one code point, both fixities, both overloads declared and in scope — parses correctly, and `⊖⊖1 ⊖ ⊖⊖2` nests it. The cost is **one `case`, 40 lines, in one file**: no Sema, AST, serialization, mangling or tooling file was touched, because U11/U13/U16 had already made every downstream API arity-generic. That is a stronger form of U§6's claim than U§6 makes — the *second fixity* of the feature is cheaper than the first operator. (2) **"Arity selects the form" is true of declarations but not of uses, and [over-oper-restrictions](#over-oper-restrictions)(b) is why.** [over.oper]p8 forbids default arguments on operator functions precisely so that declared parameter count fixes fixity; [over-oper-restrictions](#over-oper-restrictions)(b) waived it for user operators, and `CheckUserOperatorDeclaration` checks only arity. Measured: with `constexpr int operator⊟(int a, int b = 1)` and nothing else, `⊟5` is **accepted** and calls it, == 36 — a two-parameter operator used in prefix position. Add a genuine prefix overload and `⊠5` is `call to 'operator⊠' is ambiguous`, the ordinary ambiguity for `f(x)` given `f(int)` and `f(int, int = 1)`. This is not a leak and it should not be "fixed" in the parser: [candidate-assembly](../../../docs/unicode-operators.md#candidate-assembly)/§17.4 require the operator form to find exactly what the explicit call `operator⊟(5)` finds, so filtering candidates by declared arity would break the equivalence that is the whole desugaring claim. It is nevertheless live design surface — the honest choice is between keeping p8 waived and accepting dual-fixity operators, or reinstating p8 for user operators (one diagnostic) so that [unary-forms](../../../docs/unicode-operators.md#unary-forms)'s arity rule is literally true. Note the waiver was arrived at by *omission* rather than by argument. (3) **There is no postfix form and there is also no postfix diagnostic.** U08 deliberately emitted nothing at declaration time on the grounds that the forms are indistinguishable there and "a postfix *use* is diagnosed at the use site". The use site diagnoses something, but not that: position makes `a⊖` an *infix* use with its right operand missing, so the text is `error: expected expression` with the caret just past the operator — character-identical to `a +;`, and identical with or without a space (`a ⊖ ;`). Nothing mentions fixity, because by then the parser has no way to know postfix was meant. Acceptable (it is the same quality of error C++ gives for every incomplete binary expression) but it is *not* what [unary-forms](../../../docs/unicode-operators.md#unary-forms)'s "declining postfix" sentence leads a reader to expect.

**Recommended doc change.** Rewrite [unary-forms](../../../docs/unicode-operators.md#unary-forms)'s second sentence to separate the two claims it currently runs together: **arity selects the form at the point of declaration**, and **grammatical position selects it at the point of use** — and say that the second needs no help from the first, which is what makes the Swift trap avoidable. Add to U§6, after the production, the structural reason in one sentence (the prefix production is read in operand position and the infix production in operator position, by two different functions, so no disambiguation state exists — contrast §5's same-delimiter suppression flag, which backtick needs and this feature does not). Then add a short paragraph to U§7 "Declaring" on default arguments: state explicitly that [over.oper]p8 is waived (currently only [over-oper-restrictions](#over-oper-restrictions) records it), state the consequence — a defaulted trailing parameter makes an infix-declared operator prefix-usable — and take a position, noting that reinstating p8 is the conservative option and costs one diagnostic. Finally, in [unary-forms](../../../docs/unicode-operators.md#unary-forms) or U§13, say plainly what a postfix attempt actually produces, so nobody promises a diagnostic that does not exist.

### operand-sequencing

**Formerly:** `DEV-U16`. **Status:** **RECONCILED — recorded as an open CWG question**, 2026-09-06, [reconcile-declaring-using](../../completion/steps/reconcile-declaring-using.md), into `docs/unicode-operators.md` **U§7 "Desugaring"**, the second qualification: the two-case rule as two bullets (non-member — the operands are arguments, indeterminately sequenced; member — the left operand is the object expression and [expr.call] sequences it before the right), then the paragraph beginning *"So the sequencing of `x ⊞ y` is determined by overload resolution"*, which says this is a property no existing C++ operator has, takes the design's position, and asks it of CWG rather than settling it. The wholesale citation of [evaluation-order](../../../docs/backtick-operator-design.md#evaluation-order) is gone, and the closing paragraph says explicitly that the callee-before-operands sentence is **backtick-only** and must not be quoted for a Unicode operator. **This is a resolution, not a deferral:** the row has no implementation consequence and nothing to fix — what it needed was to stop being inherited by silence, and a question asked in the design is the form that takes. **Re-derived 2026-09-06** on the `unicode-operators-upstream` build: `-Wunsequenced` fires on `arr[i++] ⊩ i++` and is silent on the member form, on `objs[i++].operator⊪(i++)` and on the built-in `<<` control; and a constant-evaluation ordering probe shows the member form evaluating its left operand first. `papers/dxxxxr0.md` already lists this under *"What is not resolved"* in the same terms; it was not re-edited.

**Found by.** U14

**Design section.** [evaluation-order](../../../docs/backtick-operator-design.md#evaluation-order) / §17.2 (evaluation order), carried verbatim into U§7 "Desugaring"; U§7's "(member form: `x.operator⊞(y)`)" parenthesis

**What the design said.** [evaluation-order](../../../docs/backtick-operator-design.md#evaluation-order) says the operator "introduces **no new evaluation-order rule** and inherits [expr.call] wholesale", with two consequences stated as if they always hold: **(i)** "operand evaluation order is **unspecified** (indeterminately sequenced)" and **(ii)** "since C++17 the callee is sequenced *before* the arguments, so the **slot is evaluated before both operands**". U§7 cites both without qualification.

**What was true.** Both halves needed splitting, and neither is a Clang defect — each is a faithful consequence of "it is just the call" that the design text does not state. **(a) Claim (i) holds only for the non-member form.** For a *member* operator the left operand is the object expression, which is part of the postfix-expression, and [expr.call]p8 sequences the postfix-expression before every argument — so `x ⊞ y` **does** sequence `x` before `y` when a member overload wins. Measured three ways: `order_member() == 1122` while `order_op() ∈ {1122, 2211}` in constant evaluation; `-Wunsequenced` fires on `objs[i++] ⊪ i++`'s non-member twin but is silent on the member form and on its `.operator⊪()` desugaring; and the IR emits the object expression's call first. **Whether the left operand is sequenced first therefore depends on which overload is selected** — a property no existing C++ operator has, because [over.match.oper]p2 makes an *overloaded* built-in-spelled operator use the built-in's sequencing regardless of member-ness, and a user operator has no built-in to borrow sequencing from. The `-Wunsequenced` triple is the sharpest evidence that the operator got the call's rules and not an operator's: `i++ << i++` is silent (built-in LHS-before-RHS), `nonmember_op(i++, i++)` warns, and `i++ ⊩ i++` warns identically. **(b) Claim (ii) is vacuous for a Unicode operator.** Backtick's slot is an *expression* and can have side effects, which is what makes "the slot is evaluated first even though it is written between the operands" a surprising and useful thing to say. A Unicode operator's callee is a *name*: there is no callee subexpression, so [expr.call]p8's first sentence has nothing to sequence. Carrying [evaluation-order](../../../docs/backtick-operator-design.md#evaluation-order) over verbatim imports a sentence that cannot apply.

**Recommended doc change.** U§7 "Desugaring" should stop citing [evaluation-order](../../../docs/backtick-operator-design.md#evaluation-order) wholesale and state the two-case rule itself: *the operands are the arguments of the selected call, so for a non-member operator they are indeterminately sequenced in an unspecified order, and for a member operator the left operand is the object expression and is sequenced before the right.* Add the consequence in one sentence — **the sequencing of `x ⊞ y` is determined by overload resolution** — and say plainly that this differs from every existing operator. It is defensible (it is exactly what `x.operator⊞(y)` and `operator⊞(x, y)` respectively give, which is the [candidate-assembly](../../../docs/unicode-operators.md#candidate-assembly)/§17.4 equivalence the whole design rests on) but it must be *decided* rather than inherited by silence, because the alternative — importing [over.match.oper]p2's "sequenced in the order prescribed for the built-in operator" — has no built-in to name and so cannot be written down. Also drop or qualify the slot-before-operands sentence when [evaluation-order](../../../docs/backtick-operator-design.md#evaluation-order) is quoted in the Unicode paper: it is a backtick-only observation. Evidence: `clang/test/SemaCXX/unicode-operator-semantics.cpp` §6 and `clang/test/CodeGenCXX/unicode-operator-semantics.cpp`'s member-order block.

**Found by.** U15

**Design section.** U§8 "Implementation sketch" (the AST-node shape); U§12's "one level, banked once for both features"; backtick [desugaring-target](../../../docs/backtick-operator-design.md#desugaring-target)/§6 (the `BacktickInfixExpr` wrapper)

**What the design said.** Both features are described as "sugar for a call", and the implementation sketch treats the choice of AST node as a presentation detail: backtick wraps the `CallExpr` it built (`BacktickInfixExpr::Inner`, commented *"Always a CallExpr"*), the Unicode operator gets `UserOperatorExpr` holding its operands. The design text never suggests the two choices differ in consequence.

**What was true.** **They differ, and the backtick one is wrong.** `Sema::ActOnBacktickOperator` stores whatever `BuildCallExpr` returned, and for a class-typed result that is a `CXXBindTemporaryExpr` wrapping the `CallExpr`, not the `CallExpr`. `StmtPrinter::VisitBacktickInfixExpr` does `cast<CallExpr>(Node->getSubExpr())` and aborts. Minimal repro, no Unicode operator involved: `struct S { ~S(); }; S f(int, int); void h() { (void)(1 \`f\` 2); }` under `-fbacktick -ast-print`. A trivially-destructible class reaches it too, via `decltype`, where [expr.call]p11's deferred temporary produces the same node: `struct S {}; S f(int,int); using T = decltype(1 \`f\` 2);` aborts under `-ast-dump`. **It reproduces on the untouched `build-backtick-trunk` binary** — it is a pre-existing backtick-track defect, not a U11/U12 bug and not caused by any Unicode change; `-fsyntax-only` and codegen are unaffected, only printing. `UserOperatorExpr` has no such invariant: it stores the operands and re-forms the call, so `decltype(Res{1} ⊢ Res{2} ⊢ Res{3})` with `~Res()` non-trivial dumps and prints correctly in both fixities (§11a of `clang/test/Parser/unicode-operator-precedence.cpp`).

Add `struct S { ~S(); }` cases to `clang/test/AST/backtick-*.cpp`. **(b) Design text:** U§8 should say that a user-operator expression node holds its **operands** and not a built call, and give this as the reason rather than as a preference — a node that *is* the operator survives Sema re-wrapping its result, a node that *hides* a call does not. That is a concrete, non-obvious answer to "why is this not just a `CallExpr` with a flag", and it is worth a sentence in U§12: the two features share a precedence level and a desugaring but must **not** share an AST representation strategy. Sub-note for U18/U20: `-DPRINTING` in the precedence test exists only to route around this and should be deleted when (a) lands.

### ucn-operator-spellings

**Formerly:** `DEV-U18`. **Status:** OPEN

**Found by.** U04

**Design section.** U§8's "Two rules that fall out of single-code-point tokens", first rule ([ucn-spellings](../../../docs/unicode-operators.md#ucn-spellings)); U§5 predicate 5 / U§10 (the exclusion diagnostics U05 owns)

**What the design said.** U§8 says the UCN rule is **"structurally free: the UCN-designated code point takes the same phase-3 classification as a literal one on the lexer's existing UCN path (XID → identifier, [token-set](../../../docs/unicode-operators.md#token-set) → operator, else ill-formed), so `a\u229Eb` ≡ `a ⊞ b`"**, and [ucn-spellings](../../../docs/unicode-operators.md#ucn-spellings)'s decisions-log entry repeats "structurally free". Nothing distinguishes *lexing alike* from *meaning the same thing*.

**What was true.** **The classification really is free and really does converge — and the identity is not free, which is the finding.** (a) **Convergence, measured:** there is exactly **one** classification point in the lexer, `Lexer::isUserOperatorCodePoint` (one flag test, one binary search), with **four** callers — the direct UTF-8 decode and the UCN decode in `LexTokenInternal`, and the two identifier-*continuation* paths that must stop at an operator rather than absorb it (`tryConsumeIdentifierUTF8Char`, `tryConsumeIdentifierUCN`). The token-formation half of the UCN rule is literally two lines, because by the time control reaches `case '\\':` the UCN has already been decoded to a `uint32_t` and there is nothing spelling-specific left to decide. U§8's assertion holds and can be quantified rather than asserted. (b) **But a token is not an entity.** `Lexer::getUserOperatorCodePoint` — the identity function for the whole feature; `DeclarationName`, the Itanium mangling, `-ast-print` and the on-disk `DeclarationNameKey` all key on its result — decoded the token's *raw spelling* as a single UTF-8 scalar and answered **0** for any UCN spelling. Hooking the lexer without teaching that one function to decode UCNs would have made `operator\U0000229E` a **different entity** from `operator⊞`, with a different mangled symbol (`op_u0000`), while `-dump-tokens` output, the token kind and the token stream were all exactly right. **The step file's own gate — "all four spellings `-dump-tokens` identically" — would have passed.** The failure is silent in the one direction that matters, and the fix is one canonicalization at one function; U04 therefore asserts the rule at declaration/use level (declare with `\N{SQUARED PLUS}`, define with the glyph, one entity; one mangled symbol with `--implicit-check-not=op_u0000`; byte-identical `-ast-print` under `-DSPELL_UCN`) and not at token level. (c) **"Else ill-formed" hides a spelling-dependent diagnostic.** A code point that is neither [token-set](../../../docs/unicode-operators.md#token-set) nor XID — including every *excluded* code point, which is U05's subject — is ill-formed in both spellings, but not identically: a literal glyph gets `err_character_not_allowed` naming the code point (`unexpected character '−' U+2212`) and is then dropped, while the same code point spelled as a UCN becomes a bare `tok::unknown` with **no lexer diagnostic at all**, leaving only the parser's `expected ';'`. This is upstream's own rule and it is deliberate — `LexUnicodeIdentifierStart` may only "drop the character" when it was spelled as a literal character, because the standard forbids throwing away a possible preprocessing token that was written as an explicit UCN — so it is inherited, not introduced. Nothing is silently *accepted* in either spelling, but only one spelling gets a message about the code point.

**Recommended doc change.** Three changes. (1) In U§8, replace "structurally free" with the split: **classification is free — one helper, four callers, two lines for the UCN path — and identity is not.** Say what identity costs (one canonicalization, at the single function that turns a spelling into a scalar) and, more importantly, say *why it must be there*: the equivalence [ucn-spellings](../../../docs/unicode-operators.md#ucn-spellings) claims is an equivalence of **entities**, not of tokens, and an implementation that stops at the token gets a feature that lexes correctly and links wrongly. This is the sharpest thing the prototype has to say about UCN spellings and it generalizes to GCC (`cp_user_operator_id` must be built from the code point, not the spelling). It is also a concrete argument for U03's choice to make the *code point* the identity: because the canonicalization has exactly one home, "every spelling names one entity" is true by construction everywhere downstream rather than being re-established at each consumer. (2) Add a sentence to [ucn-spellings](../../../docs/unicode-operators.md#ucn-spellings) in the decisions log saying that the equivalence is testable only at declaration/use level — a token-stream comparison cannot see it — because that is the acceptance criterion a reviewer will otherwise write down wrongly. (3) In U§5/U§10, note the spelling-dependent diagnostic for ill-formed code points and decide whether U05's exclusion messages must fire for UCN spellings too. The security property U§10 states ("`int x = 1 − 2;` must fail, never quietly become subtraction") holds in both spellings today, but only the glyph gets told *why*; a confusable-character exclusion whose whole purpose is to protect the *reader* arguably has less to say about a UCN, which no reader can be confused by — which is a genuine argument for leaving the asymmetry alone, and worth making explicitly rather than inheriting silently.

### exclusion-diagnostics

**Formerly:** `DEV-U19`. **Status:** OPEN

**Found by.** U05

**Design section.** U§8 paragraph 2, U§5 predicate 5, U§10, U§7.1

**What the design said.** "Keep the **exclusion list as a second, tiny table with reasons**, not merely as absent entries: U+2212 in source should produce \"U+2212 MINUS SIGN is not an operator; did you mean `-`?\" — and ∂ ∇ ∞ \"is an identifier character (mathematical notation profile), not an operator\" — rather than a generic stray-character error." The three reasons are given as one list of messages emitted in one place.

**What was true.** **The table is right; the reasons do not have the same scope, and one of them cannot be emitted where the design puts it.** (a) `ConfusableWith` (13 entries) and `EmojiPresentation` (12) name code points that are *not* identifier characters and never were — Pattern_Syntax excludes them from XID by construction — so nothing but an operator could have been meant, classification is context-free, and the lexer emits the reason at the classification point. (b) `IdentifierProfile` (∂ U+2202, ∇ U+2207, ∞ U+221E) names code points that **are valid identifier characters in this compiler**: Clang's D137051 mathematical-notation extension is on by default at every `-std=` (measured by U04), so `int ∂(int); int u = ∂(1);` compiles today, with a `-Wc++2d-extensions` warning and no error. The design's message for these, emitted at the classification point, would be an **error on every legitimate use of one as a name** — it would break a shipping feature while implementing a new one, and falsify [operator-identifier-disjointness](../../../docs/unicode-operators.md#operator-identifier-disjointness)'s composability claim in the act of testing it. It is emitted instead as a **note in operator-name position only**, attached to a conversion-function-id parse that has already failed, because `using ∞ = int; struct T { operator ∞(); };` is a perfectly good conversion function that must keep working. (c) Two further measured facts about ∂ ∇ ∞. **`operator∂` — no space — is a single identifier**, so `int operator∂(S, S);` silently declares an ordinary function *named* `operator∂`; nothing in the operator-name path ever sees it. And `operator ∂` — with a space — is a conversion-function-id whose type name is unknown, which is the only context where an operator was unambiguously meant. (d) The design's "did you mean `-`?" phrasing was **not** used, and that is a table finding: the ASCII spellings U02 recorded include ∙ U+2219 → `.`, ⋅ U+22C5 → `.` and ⇔ U+21D4 → `<=>`, which are honest *confusability* claims and dishonest *intent* guesses — nobody writing `a ∙ b` meant `a . b`. The message states the confusability (`'∙' U+2219 is not a user-defined operator: it is confusable with '.'`) and no `FixItHint` is attached, so an applied fix-it can never silently change meaning.

**Recommended doc change.** Three changes to U§8 paragraph 2 and one to U§5. (1) Say that the exclusion table carries reasons **of different scope**, and name the split: a reason is lexer-emittable iff its code points cannot also be identifier constituents. That is a property of the *derivation*, not of the implementation, so the proposal can state it. (2) Replace the ∂ ∇ ∞ message with the note-in-operator-position form, and say why: the three characters are excluded from [token-set](../../../docs/unicode-operators.md#token-set) **because** TR31 §7.1 cedes them to the identifier side, so a compiler that implements both must not take them back at lex time. This is the sharpest evidence for [operator-identifier-disjointness](../../../docs/unicode-operators.md#operator-identifier-disjointness) / U§7.1 the prototype produces — the disjointness claim is not merely true on paper, it is what makes the exclusion diagnostic implementable at all. (3) Note that `operator∂` is one identifier: a future proposal that admits [token-set](../../../docs/unicode-operators.md#token-set) code points into identifiers would break this, which is a second, independent argument for keeping the sets disjoint. (4) In U§5, replace "did you mean X?" with "is confusable with X" and record that no fix-it is offered, since the exclusion list contains entries whose ASCII twin is a confusability claim rather than a plausible intent.

### clang-format-user-operators

**Formerly:** `DEV-U20`. **Status:** OPEN

**Found by.** U18

**Design section.** U§10 (the clang-format bullet in "Objections and answers"); U§11's open-question list ("`operator` + token adjacency … and what clang-format canonicalizes"); [user-declared-fixity](../../../docs/unicode-operators.md#user-declared-fixity) (one fixed level)

**What the design said.** "**clang-format** treats a user operator as a binary/unary operator token at the fixed level — no §7-style delimiter pairing, no break-suppression zones. Strictly less work than backtick's formatting story." Read as: annotate the token and the formatter does the rest.

**What was true.** **The claim holds and is now quantified — but it is three touch points, not one, and the third is invisible to every spacing test.** Measured: **3 production files, +32/−9**, no new `TokenType`, no annotator state variable, no custom spacing rule, no custom break rule, no style option — against backtick's 4 new `TT_` types, a `PendingBacktickKind` state field, bespoke `spaceRequiredBetween` rules and bespoke `canBreakBefore` rules (S10). (a) Enabling is one line, `LangOpts.UnicodeOperators = 1` beside S10's `LangOpts.Backtick = 1` in `getFormattingLangOpts()`, under the same `LK_Cpp || LK_ObjC` guard. (b) Classification is four lines in `determineTokenType`: `TT_BinaryOperator` after an operand, `TT_UnaryOperator` otherwise. Position alone sufficed — no lookahead, no declaration lookup, no suppression flag — so [unary-forms](../../../docs/unicode-operators.md#unary-forms)/U12's parser result transfers verbatim to the formatter, and `operator⊞` needed **no rule at all**: the existing `kw_operator` handler rewrites the unary annotation to `TT_OverloadedOperator`, the same path `operator +` takes. (c) **`FormatToken::getPrecedence()` must also be told.** It calls `getBinOpPrecedence` with hard-coded arguments, so `tok::user_operator` answered `prec::Unknown`; `ExpressionParser::getCurrentPrecedence` then builds no fake-parenthesis structure for a user-operator chain, and wrapping a long chain is wrong **while every spacing and annotation test passes**. Backtick never met this because `tok::backtick`'s precedence takes no flag argument. Two further measurements. **The open question is answered:** `operator ⊞` canonicalizes to `operator⊞` — the space is removed, governed by the existing `SpaceAfterOperatorKeyword` option, exactly as for `operator +`. **And the Unicode operator gets a formatting capability backtick does not:** `BreakBeforeBinaryOperators: All` puts the break before `⊞`, because it really is a `TT_BinaryOperator`; backtick's [format-break-policy](../../../docs/backtick-operator-design.md#format-break-policy) rules hard-forbid breaks adjacent to its delimiters, so the option cannot apply to `` x `f` y ``. Finally, enabling the feature in the format path changes the *token stream* for **excluded** code points (U+2212 now stops an identifier instead of being absorbed into it, since U05's hook is `LangOpts`-gated and not raw-mode-gated) but **not the output**: `FormatTokenLexer` types every `tok::unknown` as `TT_ImplicitStringLiteral`, whose rule is "preserve the surrounding whitespace", so such source formats byte-identically to the pre-change binary — measured, not assumed.

**Recommended doc change.** Three edits. (1) In U§10, keep the bullet and add the measurement — "three touch points in clang-format: enable the lexer flag, annotate the token as binary/unary by position, and give it a precedence; ~30 lines, no new token type and no state machine" — and say the third is the one a reviewer will forget, because omitting it breaks *wrapping* while leaving *spacing* correct. That is a concrete argument for [user-declared-fixity](../../../docs/unicode-operators.md#user-declared-fixity) (one fixed level): the formatter needs a precedence for the token, and there is exactly one to give it. (2) Delete the clang-format half of U§11's open question and state the answer: `operator ⊞` and `operator⊞` both parse and clang-format canonicalizes to the closed form, following `operator+`. (3) Worth a sentence in U§12 or wherever the two features are compared: the Unicode operator inherits `BreakBeforeBinaryOperators` and ordinary split penalties because it *is* a binary operator token, whereas backtick's delimiter pair needs — and gets — break suppression instead. The formatting story is not merely cheaper, it is better-behaved.

### postfix-operators

**Formerly:** `DEV-U23`. **Status:** **OPEN — substance DECIDED 2026-09-06; mangling clause RECONCILED 2026-09-06 into U§9's [abi-production-request](../../../docs/unicode-operators.md#abi-production-request); the remaining doc changes unwritten.** [postfix-operators](../../../docs/open-decisions.md#postfix-operators), option (a): postfix is **declined for v1 and explicitly not foreclosed**, argued in the *affordable and declined* terms rather than the *ambiguous* terms, because greedy-infix only ever reinterprets programs v1 rejects. U§13.1 keeps its full subsection: a priced negative result is the point, not an appendix. Still `OPEN` on one count: this row's recommended doc changes (1), (4) and (5) are unwritten ([reconcile-remainder](../../completion/steps/reconcile-remainder.md)'s U§13, and the U§12 routing consequence). **Clause (3), the mangling clause, is taken and closed** — [mangling-abi](../../completion/steps/mangling-abi.md) wrote it on 2026-09-06 into [`docs/unicode-operators.md`](../../../docs/unicode-operators.md) **U§9's [abi-production-request](../../../docs/unicode-operators.md#abi-production-request)**, in two places: the **third "What was measured" bullet** (*`v <digit>` keys on arity, and arity is not fixity*), which is the clause's substance — that the vendor-extended form cannot tell prefix from postfix and would foreclose v2 if it became the standardized encoding — and **point 1 of the Recommendation** (*The fixity marker is the whole point of asking*), which is what the paper would ask the ABI group for and links back to this row. **Found by.** U21

**Design section.** [unary-forms](../../../docs/unicode-operators.md#unary-forms) (no postfix, "declining postfix eliminates the prefix/postfix ambiguity"); U§13 (open questions); [operator-mangling](../../../docs/unicode-operators.md#operator-mangling)/U§9 (mangling, open); [operator-function-id](../../../docs/unicode-operators.md#operator-function-id) (no class-or-enum requirement)

**What the design said.** [unary-forms](../../../docs/unicode-operators.md#unary-forms)'s rationale reads as *postfix is ambiguous, therefore excluded*, and the exclusion is justified by the Swift whitespace trap. U§13 carried no postfix entry at all. The U20 forward notes predicted that "prefix and postfix would mangle identically under U09's `v<arity>` scheme", i.e. that the mangling collision is a consequence of taking postfix.

**What was true.** **Three corrections, all measured on the prototype branch.** (a) **The ambiguity is resolvable without whitespace sensitivity.** A one-token greedy-infix rule — after a complete operand, a user operator followed by a token that can begin a cast-expression is infix, else postfix — was prototyped in `Parser::ParsePostfixExpressionSuffix` in **68 lines, one file**, with one `NextToken()` call, no backtracking and no declaration lookup ([lexing-and-declarations](../../../docs/unicode-operators.md#lexing-and-declarations) intact). It parses `a ⊖;`, `(a ⊖) ⊗`, `a ⊖ / a` as postfix and leaves `a ⊞ b` untouched. So [unary-forms](../../../docs/unicode-operators.md#unary-forms)'s premise is too strong: the correct statement is not "postfix is ambiguous" but "postfix is affordable and we are declining to spend it". (b) **The step file's paren-forcing set is wrong in its specifics.** It predicted "exactly the prefix-unary ∩ infix-binary tokens". Measured against `ParseCastExpression`'s 135-label token switch (`ParseExpr.cpp`, whose `default:` sets `NotCastExpr`), the real set also contains `++`, `--`, `(`, `[` and — the one that matters — **`&&`**, which is a cast-expression starter in every language mode because of the GNU address-of-label extension (`ParseExpr.cpp`, `case tok::ampamp`, ungated). `a ⊖ && b` misparses as `⊖(a, &&b)` and diagnoses `use of undeclared label 'b'`. Curating `&&` onto the terminator side is possible but converts the rule from a derivation into a normative hand-written token list — and the compiler's own answer to "can this token begin an expression" is **language-mode dependent** (`^^` gated on reflection, `[` on C++/ObjC, `^` on blocks), so a derived predicate would make the *fixity* of an expression depend on the dialect. That is a fresh violation of [user-declared-fixity](../../../docs/unicode-operators.md#user-declared-fixity)'s invariant and is the strongest single argument against the rule. (c) **The mangling prediction is right about the collision and wrong about its inevitability, and it uncovered a live Clang defect.** Prefix and postfix unaries do share an arity, so `v1op_uXXXX` is ambiguous — but the Itanium ABI already solves this for `++`: `<expression>` spells prefix as `pp_` and postfix as `pp` (`llvm/include/llvm/Demangle/ItaniumDemangle.h`, and its parser implements the `_`). **GCC 15.2 emits both forms; Clang emits `pp` for both**, so `template<class T> void f(decltype(++T{})); template<class T> void f(decltype(T{}++));` is `error: definition with same mangled name '_Z1fI1AEvDTpptlT_EE'` on Clang and two distinct symbols on GCC. A cross-vendor divergence found by accident, and independent of this feature. **Reported upstream as LLVM-ISSUE-PENDING** — [upstream-reports](../../completion/steps/upstream-reports.md) re-confirmed it against trunk `72417eb739e5` on 2026-09-06, for `--` as well as `++`, and wrote the [draft](../../completion/upstream-drafts/increment-decrement-mangling.md); the author's decision is that the maintainer files it, so the number is a placeholder until then and [`increment-decrement-mangling`](../../BACKLOG.md#increment-decrement-mangling) stays open. Two corrections from that confirmation: the reproducer needs explicit instantiations to force emission, and the ABI citation is §5.1.3 (`<operator-name> ::= pp # ++ (postfix in <expression> context)`) together with §5.1.6 (`<expression> ::= pp_ <expression> # prefix ++`). For user operators the escape is to synthesize the `std::postfix` tag as a real first argument (`CreateOverloadedUnaryOp`'s `IntegerLiteral 0` trick, generalized), which makes the postfix call 2-ary and mangles it distinctly with no ABI change — at the cost of a fixity bit on `UserOperatorExpr`, since `NumOperands` no longer recovers it. **A fourth measured cost the design did not anticipate at all:** greedy-infix turns every missing-right-operand typo into a valid postfix parse, so `a ⊞ ;` stops diagnosing `expected expression` and starts diagnosing `no matching function for call to 'operator⊞'`. **Four existing negative tests change behaviour**, all of that shape, two of them fold expressions that cascade into `expected ')'` + `expression contains unexpanded parameter pack`. That cost falls on every user of the feature, not only on postfix users. **And the forward-compatibility result that decides it:** greedy-infix fires only where a user operator is followed by a token that cannot begin a cast-expression, and v1 requires a cast-expression there — so every program the rule reinterprets is a program v1 rejects. Structurally, and measured: nothing well-formed changed, only diagnostics on already-ill-formed code.

**Recommended doc change.** (1) **Rewrite [unary-forms](../../../docs/unicode-operators.md#unary-forms)'s rationale** (done in this repo): replace "declining postfix eliminates the ambiguity" with "postfix is a pure extension we can decline for free", and carry the four prices. The distinction matters in the room — "we couldn't" invites a fix, "we chose not to, and here is what it costs and why it stays possible" does not. (2) **Add U§13.1** (done): the full finding with witnesses, the `&&` case, the `pp_`/`pp` measurement, and the recommendation. (3) **[operator-mangling](../../../docs/unicode-operators.md#operator-mangling) gains a second reason to stay open**, alongside U20's: a first-class `<operator-name>` production would need room for a fixity marker that `v <digit> <source-name>` does not have. (4) **Record the routing consequence in U§12 or [library-scope](../../../docs/backtick-operator-design.md#library-scope)'s terms**: a compiler-known `std::postfix` is library-affects-language (precedent `operator<=>`/`std::strong_ordering`, which costs Clang 452 lines of dedicated AST support across 26 files), so postfix would add **LEWG** to a paper already going to SG16, EWG/CWG and the ABI group — which [library-scope](../../../docs/backtick-operator-design.md#library-scope)'s own bundling rule says keeps it out of v1. (5) Note the `operator++` finding as a fourth instance of the "sibling, not widening" pattern (U08, U13, U16, now U21): postfix-ness has **no representation** in Clang — no `isPostfix()` on `CXXOperatorCallExpr`, no "can begin an expression" predicate anywhere — and is re-derived at each consumer from `OO_PlusPlus` plus an argument count.

**Found by.** BL04

**Design section.** [expression-node-cost](#expression-node-cost)'s 28-site dispatch count; U§8's Clang bullet list (the code-generation cost)

**What the design said.** [expression-node-cost](#expression-node-cost) counted **28 dispatch sites over `Stmt::StmtClass` in 20 files** and treated the list as complete, sorting them into 6 forced by a link error, 8 by an exhaustive `switch` ending in `llvm_unreachable`, 1 found only by reading a `-Wswitch` warning, and 13 silent. [serialization-tooling-cost](#serialization-tooling-cost) raised the `-Wswitch` category to 2 and the silent one to 13. Neither list contains a ClangIR site, because `clang/lib/CIR/` was never built: all four build dirs are `LLVM_ENABLE_PROJECTS=clang;clang-tools-extra`, and six consecutive steps flagged it unexamined. [`clangir-unicode-arms`](../../BACKLOG.md#clangir-unicode-arms) further recorded that the CIR fallbacks were `errorNYI` "rather than crashes", so the worst case was a hard diagnostic.

**What was true.** **Built it, and the count is 32 in 21 files — plus a fourth failure category the taxonomy did not have.** With `mlir` and `CLANG_ENABLE_CIR=ON` (the CMake `FATAL_ERROR`s without both), a `UserOperatorExpr` reaches four ClangIR sites, and **each fails differently**: `ScalarExprEmitter` → `errorNYI` **naming the node**; `AggExprEmitter` → `errorNYI` naming the node; `ComplexExprEmitter` → `errorUnsupported`, `"cannot compile this complex expression yet"`, which **does not name the node** and would be the hardest of the four to attribute in the field; `CIRGenFunction::emitLValue` → `errorNYI "unsupported l-value class"` **and then an assertion failure**, `!isNull() && "Cannot retrieve a NULL type pointer"` in `QualType::getCommonPtr`, because the default arm returns a default-constructed `LValue`. So [`clangir-unicode-arms`](../../BACKLOG.md#clangir-unicode-arms)'s "not crashes" is wrong for one site in four, and **the crash is not the node's**: `BacktickInfixExpr` aborts identically at the same site on the backtick branches, which localizes it to the default arm rather than to either wrapper. (a) **The new category is "forced, but only at run time, and only if someone builds it".** [expression-node-cost](#expression-node-cost)'s taxonomy sorted sites by whether the *build* forces them. These four are not build-forced at all — nothing failed to compile — yet three of them fail loudly the first time a use is compiled, and one aborts. They are neither the forced half nor the silent half; they are latent, and what made them latent was a CMake default, not a language or a visitor design. **The generalisation for the paper is that a new expression node's obligations are bounded by the *configuration* of the tree you measure in, not by the tree** — and a second, out-of-tree code generator is exactly where that bites. (b) **Three arms are the predicted copy-paste and the fourth is a genuine design answer.** `emitLValue`'s `CXXRewrittenBinaryOperator` arm diagnoses NYI, and copying it would have been wrong: an operator returning a reference *is* a call returning a reference, and the `CallExpr` classes four lines above already handle it. The wrapper arms therefore recurse (`emitLValue(getSemanticForm())`) where the model diagnoses. The upstream node is the better model in three places and the wrong one in the fourth. (c) **The desugaring thesis survives its last test.** Every shape emits CIR *instruction-for-instruction identical* to the explicit call written out by hand — scalar, aggregate, complex, l-value and prefix — including the `cir.store` through the pointer an l-value-returning operator returns. This is the furthest downstream the claim has been checked on either feature.

**Recommended doc change.** Add the ClangIR sites to [expression-node-cost](#expression-node-cost)'s count in U§8 — **32 over `StmtClass` in 21 files, not 28 in 20** — and add the fourth category with it, because the category is the finding rather than the four. Say plainly that the toolchain forces roughly half a new node's obligations, warns about two, is silent about thirteen, and **hides four behind a build configuration nobody in the project had turned on**; the last group is the one a vendor prototyping a language change is most likely to ship without. Then use (c) in U§7 "Desugaring": the paper can now say the sugar and the call agree at the IR level, not only in the AST and in overload resolution. Finally, `emitLValue`'s crash-on-default is worth one sentence as an *upstream* observation independent of either feature — a default arm that returns a null-typed `LValue` turns every missing l-value class into an abort rather than a diagnostic — and BL05's report is the precedent for what to do with such a thing.
